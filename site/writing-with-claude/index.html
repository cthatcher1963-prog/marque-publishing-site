The book

The Writing Assistant He Couldn't Trust

The first time Chris let Claude write his novel, it produced seventy-five thousand words overnight. Competent, complete, and not his. The manuscript went into an archive folder, and what came out of the wreckage was a system: a way of working with AI that keeps the author in the chair and the machine on a leash.

The book walks that system from the first blank page to a book on the shelf. A thesis that survives interrogation. A scaffold you build before a word of prose exists. Rules that load every session, because a partner with no memory has to be briefed every time. A voice profile that teaches Claude to sound like you. A five-pass edit machine. And a production run that gets from finished draft to Amazon in days instead of months.

"Policy without enforcement is a recommendation."

It is written by someone who spent thirty years in cybersecurity and applied the one doctrine he trusted — never trust, always verify — to the most productive, most confident, least reliable collaborator he has ever worked with.
