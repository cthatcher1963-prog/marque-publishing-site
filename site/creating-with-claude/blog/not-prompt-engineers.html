Why the Best AI Creators Will Never Call Themselves Prompt Engineers

Prompt engineering is real. It matters. And it's not the skill that determines who builds remarkable things with AI.

The term "prompt engineer" centers the technology. It implies the hard part is crafting the right input — finding the magic words that unlock the AI's capabilities. Get the prompt right, the thinking goes, and the output takes care of itself.

I've built enough projects to know that's backward.

The hard part is never the prompt. The hard part is knowing what to build, understanding who it's for, recognizing when the output is wrong in ways that aren't obvious, and having the judgment to push through the messy middle of a project where nothing works quite right and the temptation to ship "good enough" is overwhelming.

Those are creator skills, not engineering skills. They come from domain expertise, taste, experience, and the kind of stubbornness that keeps you iterating when the fourth version still isn't right.

The best people I've watched use AI — across industries, across experience levels — share a common trait: they know their domain deeply and they refuse to accept output that doesn't meet their standard, no matter how fluent it sounds. They treat AI output the way a good editor treats a first draft: as raw material, not a finished product.

This is a critical distinction for anyone thinking about their career in the next five years. If you've invested your energy in becoming a prompt engineer and nothing else, your skill has a short shelf life. The models get better at understanding imprecise inputs every quarter. The prompting tricks that work today will be unnecessary tomorrow.

But if you've invested your energy in becoming genuinely excellent at something — marketing, analysis, design, writing, cybersecurity, medicine, teaching, anything — and then learned how to bring that expertise into AI collaboration, you have a compounding advantage. Your domain knowledge doesn't depreciate. It appreciates, because AI makes it more powerful every year.

The best AI creators ten years from now won't be the people who mastered prompting. They'll be the people who mastered something real and learned to amplify it.

That's the bet I made when I wrote the book, and it's the bet I'd encourage you to make with your career.
