I Wrote a Book With AI Without Letting AI Write the Book

This is the question I get most, and I understand why it makes people uneasy.

"So did Claude write your book?"

No. And the distinction isn't semantic. It's the whole point.

Claude didn't write my book for the same reason a table saw doesn't build a cabinet. The tool does work. The craftsman makes decisions. The decisions are what make it a cabinet instead of a pile of boards.

Every idea in the book is mine — earned from thirty years of work and two years of intensive building with AI. Every argument has my fingerprints on it. Every sentence was approved, revised, or rewritten by me until it said what I meant, the way I'd say it.

What Claude did was make me faster, sharper, and more thorough than I could have been alone. It helped me think through structures I might have missed. It caught inconsistencies I'd read past. It generated first drafts that I then rewrote, sometimes entirely, until they were mine.

The difference between "AI wrote this" and "I wrote this with AI" isn't about what percentage of the words came from which source. It's about who's driving. When AI is driving, the output sounds like AI — fluent, confident, and interchangeable with a thousand other outputs on the same topic. When a human is driving, the output sounds like that human, because every creative decision ran through their specific judgment, experience, and taste.

I know what "AI wrote this" reads like because I've thrown away pages of it. Paragraphs that were technically fine and completely lifeless. Arguments that were logical but missed the point. Structures that made sense on paper but didn't feel right when I read them out loud.

The craft is in knowing the difference. In reading AI output and hearing your own voice — or not hearing it, and knowing you need to push harder.

This is the part that surprises people: using AI well as a creative collaborator is more work than they expect, not less. Not more work than writing alone — more work than they imagined AI collaboration would be. Because collaboration requires judgment at every step. Accept this, push back on that, rewrite this entirely, keep that but move it.

The result is better than what I would have produced alone. Significantly better. Not because the AI is a better writer than I am — it isn't, for my topics. Because having a tireless collaborator who generates material for me to react to, challenge, and reshape makes my own thinking sharper.

That's the method I teach in the book. Not "how to get AI to write for you." How to write with AI in a way that produces work that's unmistakably yours — and better than what you'd have made without it.
