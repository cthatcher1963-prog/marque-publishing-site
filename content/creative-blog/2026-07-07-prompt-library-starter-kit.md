Here's something I noticed after months of daily AI work: my best results almost never came from a prompt I wrote on the spot. They came from prompts I'd already refined three or four times — saved, reused, and improved.

Most people treat every AI conversation like a first date. You explain who you are, what you do, what you want, how you want it formatted... and then tomorrow you do it all again. The fix isn't clever prompting. It's a library.

## What's in the starter kit

The Prompt Library Starter Kit is the collection I wish someone had handed me on day one: proven prompts for the work most of us actually do — writing and editing, research, planning, summarizing, preparing for meetings — each with the reasoning behind why it's structured the way it is.

That last part matters. Copying a prompt gets you one good result. Understanding why it works gets you a hundred, because you'll start adapting the patterns to problems nobody wrote a template for.

## How to use it

Don't read it cover to cover. Pick the two or three prompts closest to your daily work, use them this week, and tweak what doesn't sound like you. A prompt library isn't something you download — it's something you grow. This just gives you a running start.
